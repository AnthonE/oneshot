# Game feel and feedback: what a shooter shows and plays in the ~500 ms around a shot, a hit, a shield break, a kill and taking damage

Researcher's note on reliability (read first). Research date 2026-11-09. Each finding below carries the source it came from. Sources are graded in the text: **[primary]** = the developer's own post, talk listing or interview; **[press]** = journalism quoting developers; **[secondary]** = guides, community or small-studio blogs (rule-of-thumb value only). Several pages could not be opened this session (GDC Vault videos, Audiokinetic's blog, PC Gamer's Halo page, two PDFs); where a finding rests on a search excerpt rather than the opened page it says "(excerpt)". Things I know of from general background but could **not** confirm from a source this session are kept out of "Cited Findings" and listed under "Gaps" as *unverified recollection*, so the writer can decide whether to use them.

## 1. Firing feedback: muzzle flash, viewmodel kick, camera recoil, shake, tracers, shells, weapon sound, haptics; what makes a gun feel like a "pea shooter"

### Takeaway
The published consensus is that a gun's perceived power is carried mostly by its sound (a sharp transient, a body, a short low-end thud, and an environment tail, with the player's own gun mixed "punchier" than everyone else's) and by a response that starts on the very next frame after the click. Camera motion is the element developers most often have to restrain: screen-centre-moving "visual recoil" and first-person shake are repeatedly reported as disorienting or sickening, so competitive games keep the camera honest and put the violence into the viewmodel, HUD, sound and muzzle effects.

### Cited Findings

Sound of the shot
- Halo Infinite builds each weapon from four layers: **Impact, Mech, Sci-fi and Tails**. The Impact layer "gives you the initial transient smack" and the "low end thud", and is "a grounding element to build upon"; Tails are unique per environment (open fields, interiors of different sizes) so "your gun fire will sound slightly different" by place (Kyle Fraser, Lead Sound Designer, 343, March 2021) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite, March 2021](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)
- Halo Infinite shortened impacts on burst weapons on purpose: "Impacts are much shorter, which helps in being able to hear the distinct 3 shots clearly" (Fraser, 2021) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite, March 2021](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)
- First-person versus third-person mix: other players' weapons in Halo Infinite have "slightly less punch and detail and a bit snappier" delivery than the player's own, and come in "close, mid and far versions" (Fraser, 2021) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite, March 2021](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)
- 343's stated goal after Halo 5: deliver "the key combat sounds in a clear and impactful way", which required *cutting* sound density; the mix can hold "50-100+ sounds playing at the same time" (Sotaro Tojima, Audio Director; Fraser; 2021) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite, March 2021](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)
- 343 reported that technical-preview players said the weapons "sounded powerful and really added to the experience"; the audio team says it worked to make gunfire "impactful" in a "clear soundscape" (343, c. Sept 2021) **[primary]** — [Technical Preview Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/technical-preview-outcomes)
- Apex Legends: "Each weapon has at least four layers of sounds that change over distance, from extremely close to very distant"; weapons were reworked to hold up at about three times Titanfall 2's range; weapons are also filtered with DSP EQ "as the sound moves closer and farther" and by whether the gun points at the listener (Erik Kraber, Respawn, 17 Apr 2019) **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- Apex added shot-to-shot and context variety so a gun fired thousands of times would not "feel stale or gamey", while keeping each gun's personality; over 16 acoustic environments (six interior, 10+ exterior) were modelled for tails (Kraber, 2019) **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- Titanfall (2014) got its DSP-like results from "many dynamic samples and many layers of sounds" because it lacked in-game reverb/EQ (Kraber, quoted 2014) **[press]** — [Post Magazine, Audio for Games, March 2014](https://www.postmagazine.com/Publications/Post-Magazine/2014/March-1-2014/Audio-For-Games.aspx)
- Why a gun sounds weak: "a weak weapon usually has a missing layer or layers fighting each other"; a strong body with no transient sounds muffled, a sharp transient with no body sounds thin and toy-like; punch comes from a sharp transient over a deeper body plus a *short* sub for weight; light weapons keep the sub light so they feel fast, shotguns/heavies get more body, sub and a longer tail; keep the dry shot separate from the tail so the engine can add the room **[secondary, sound-design guide]** — [Violet Recording: How to design weapon sounds](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-weapon-sounds/)
- Small timing offsets between the transient and body layers noticeably change perceived size and punch; clipping before the limiter keeps the transient sharp **[secondary, sound designer's blog]** — [Lior Ortega: First-person shooter gun sound design](https://www.liortega.com/post/first-person-shooter-gun-sound-design)
- A common layer breakdown in the trade: mechanics, low end, close detail, far detail, tail **[secondary]** — [Splice: How to design a weapon sound for video games](https://splice.com/blog/?p=16617)
- DICE's HDR audio (Battlefield: Bad Company, GDC 2009, Anders Clerwall): every sound is tagged with a loudness and a moving loudness window scales the rest, so loud guns push quiet sounds out; it is "not compression"; loudness doubles as the culling/priority rule because in an FPS loud is a reasonable proxy for important **[primary]** — [Frostbite: How HDR Audio Makes Battlefield: Bad Company Go BOOM](https://www.ea.com/frostbite/news/how-hdr-audio-makes-battlefield-bad-company-go-boom)
- A known HDR failure: a loud impact can hold the window too long, so the decay sounds unnaturally constant (2013 analysis) **[secondary]** — [Designing Sound: HDR audio in Wwise](https://designingsound.org/2013/06/21/finding-your-way-with-high-dynamic-range-audio-in-wwise/)
- Blizzard tried HDR audio for Overwatch and dropped it for an importance system that always "hears" what matters to the player at that moment (excerpt) **[primary, Audiokinetic write-up]** — [Audiokinetic: Overwatch game audio using Wwise, part 1](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/blog/overwatch-game-audio-using-wwise-1/)

First frame of response; muzzle flash and tracer
- Valorant: muzzle flash and tracer are client-predicted and appear at the earliest one frame after the input; Riot will not delay the bullet for the sake of visual agreement because "delaying the bullet adds input latency which we want to minimize" (Kevin Lee, Riot, 6 Aug 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)
- A tracer that shares its colour with the impact sparks is hard to follow (a Riot developer's argument during the 2020 hit-reg debate) **[press]** — [Dexerto: Riot respond to hit reg issues](https://dexerto.com/valorant/riot-games-respond-to-major-valorant-hit-reg-issues-1367017)
- CS2 treats the player's own tracers as optional noise: an option to disable first-person bullet tracers arrived in the 6 Feb 2024 "A Call to Arms" update (others still see them), and a release note says local first-person tracers now "appear to come from the weapon muzzle more reliably" **[press/secondary]** — [Dust2.us: A Call to Arms update](https://www.dust2.us/news/44353/cs2-a-call-to-arms-update-most-important-changes); [csdb.gg: disabling bullet tracers](https://csdb.gg/how-to-disable-bullet-tracers-in-cs2)

Camera recoil versus visual-only recoil; shake

- Destiny on PC: Bungie found controller recoil "felt good" and mouse recoil did not; mouse recoil for some archetypes was cut 40% relative to controller, later narrowed to 20% ahead of crossplay (2019–2021) **[press]** — [PC Gamer: Destiny 2 3.1 patch notes](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/destiny-2-3.1-patch-notes-seasons-of-the-chosen); [CharlieIntel: recoil ahead of crossplay](https://www.charlieintel.com/destiny/bungie-to-address-destiny-2-weapon-recoil-issues-ahead-of-crossplay-80935/)
- First person tolerates camera shake badly: an Obsidian combat designer said camera shake in first person "makes the game too disorienting", so the UI team made the **HUD** shake instead (Avowed) **[press]** — [PC Gamer: Avowed hitstop](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/if-youve-been-worried-about-avowed-not-looking-punchy-enough-obsidians-combat-designer-has-a-hitstop-animation-superpower-that-might-reassure-you)
- Vlambeer's rule from 2D: shake the screen "slightly in the opposite direction of the player's gunfire" (Jan Willem Nijman, "The Art of Screenshake", Control Conference, Amsterdam, Nov 2013) **[press]** — [Game Developer: Vlambeer co-founder's advice](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)
- Rule-of-thumb per-shot shake budgets from a small-studio guide (not a major developer; treat as a starting point only): pistol 1–2 px for 50 ms; assault rifle 2–3 px, 40 ms per shot; shotgun 5–8 px, 100 ms; sniper 3–5 px with ~300 ms recovery sway; always ship a "reduce screen shake" option (StraySpark, 1 Apr 2026) **[secondary]** — [StraySpark: FPS design fundamentals](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fps-game-design-fundamentals-ue5)
- Recoil recovery curve: linear recovery "feels mechanical", ease-out "feels natural" (StraySpark, 2026) **[secondary]** — [StraySpark: FPS design fundamentals](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fps-game-design-fundamentals-ue5)

Viewmodel
- Destiny draws the first-person arms/weapon with a second camera that has its own field of view (lesson citing David Helsby's GDC 2015 talk) **[secondary]** — [CG Cookie: tips for FPS animation](https://cgcookie.com/lessons/general-tips-for-any-fps-animation)
- Helsby's talk treats the camera as central to why Destiny feels good: sway modelled on a boxer's head-first movement, an aim point lower on screen than other shooters, and animation tuned to be physical without causing motion sickness (GDC 2015) **[press; talk listing primary]** — [Game Rant: how Bungie made Destiny smooth](https://gamerant.com/destiny-smooth-fps-boxing/); [GDC Vault: The Art of First Person Animation for Destiny](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022297/The-Art-of-First-Person)
- Modern Warfare (2019): an "Active Idle" gives the held weapon sway and heaviness; reloads vary with rounds left and can be done while aiming so the gun stays on target (Mark Grigsby, Animation Director, 2019) **[primary/press]** — [Activision: Modern Warfare initial intel on animation](https://blog.activision.com/call-of-duty/2019-07/Modern-Warfare-Initial-Intel-Detailing-Advancements-in-Animation-and-Authenticity); [GamesRadar: MW animation director](https://www.gamesradar.com/call-of-duty-modern-warfare-animation-director-details-the-reboots-slick-gun-animations)
- "A huge part of the tuning process commonly overlooked in FPS development is how a weapon reacts to in-game actions" (H1Z1 developer, 2014) **[press]** — [Engadget: how feel trumps realism in H1Z1](https://www.engadget.com/2014-09-29-how-feel-trumps-realism-in-h1z1s-weapon-design.html)

Haptics
- A CHI 2021 study (Singhal and Schneider, "Juicy Haptic Design") found that the *presence* of vibrotactile feedback, whether lightly or heavily "juiced", improved player-experience measures **[academic]** — [Waterloo Haptic Experience Lab: Juicy Haptic Design](https://uwaterloo.ca/haptic-experience-lab/projects/juicy-haptic-design-vibrotactile-embellishments-can-improve)

### Inferences
- "Pea shooter" is, on this evidence, first an audio diagnosis (missing transient, missing short low-end thud, or layers masking each other; or the player's own gun not mixed above third-person guns) and second a response-latency one (anything other than flash + sound + kick on the frame after the click). Damage numbers are not part of either.
- For a ranked arena shooter the defensible split is: camera rotation only where it is real gameplay recoil; everything else (kick, roll, sway, flash, shells) lives on the viewmodel layer or HUD so the crosshair never lies. BF2042's episode shows what happens when the sight, the screen centre and the bullets disagree.
- Burst and automatic weapons need *short* per-shot bodies so each round is countable by ear; long bodies smear a three-round burst into one sound (343's reasoning), which matters in a long-TTK game where players count hits.
- Checklist (present / absent / weak):
  - Shot sound has distinct transient, body, short sub, mechanical and environment tail layers; own gun is punchier than others'.
  - Per-shot variation (pitch/sample) so sustained fire is not a loop.
  - Flash, sound and viewmodel kick all start on the frame after input; nothing waits on the server.
  - Viewmodel on its own camera/FOV; kick has fast attack and eased (not linear) recovery.
  - No camera motion that moves the aim point apart from real recoil; any shake is small, short, opposite to fire, and has an off switch.
  - Own tracers come from the muzzle, are a different colour from impact sparks, and can be turned off.
  - Third-person guns have close/mid/far variants, and are filtered by distance and by whether they point at the listener.
  - Rumble on fire for pads.

### Gaps
- No developer source with **numbers for muzzle flash** (duration in ms/frames, brightness, sprite randomisation), **FOV punch**, **shell ejection** or **tracer speed/first-person offset** was found; these are covered only in tutorials. The Vlambeer talk itself (video) is the usual reference for flash and shells.
- No published per-shot shake limits from Riot, Valve, 343 or Respawn were found; the only numbers are StraySpark's rule of thumb.
- *Unverified recollection:* Nijman's talk is widely summarised as roughly 30 steps including bigger bullets, a one-frame muzzle flash, faster bullets, random spread, impact effects, enemy hit flash and knockback, permanence (corpses, shells), camera lerp and lead, screen shake, player knockback, a few tens of ms of "sleep" on hit, gun kick, "more bass", and camera kick. I confirmed only the two items cited above; the full list should be checked against the video (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U) before being quoted.
- *Unverified recollection:* Squirrel Eiserloh's GDC talk on camera shake ("trauma" squared, rotational shake preferred in 3D) is the usual technical reference; not retrieved.
- I found no first-party statement from Infinity Ward on shot audio (the MW2019 audio lead's interviews were not retrieved).

## 2. Hit confirmation: hitmarkers, hit sounds, impact effects, flinch on the target, damage numbers, and how late they can arrive

### Takeaway
Well-regarded shooters confirm a hit on several channels at once (a reticle marker, a dedicated non-diegetic hit sound, and an effect on the target that says *where* and *on what* the hit landed), and sound designers openly trade realism for that feedback. The best-documented failure is not missing feedback but *misleading* feedback: Riot found that most "bad hit reg" reports were clarity problems caused by a server-confirmed effect arriving one round trip late at a stale world position, and both Riot and Valve now split feedback into an instant client-predicted layer and a server-confirmed layer.

### Cited Findings

The hit sound
- Overwatch's "hit-pip" is a reversed, tweaked recording of a beer bottle being opened; the design brief was that it "needs to be apparent, but not distracting. Satisfyingly powerful, but not overwhelming" (Paul Lackey, sound design supervisor, Blizzard, 2016) (excerpt) **[press quoting the Collector's Edition source book]** — [Shacknews: Overwatch's hit sound](https://shacknews.com/article/94821/overwatchs-hit-sound-effect-comes-from-cracking-open-a-cold-one)
- A 2016 criticism of Overwatch's scheme: it "could use a bit more differentiation in sound between types of hits" (excerpt) **[press]** — [Kotaku: the sound of a hit in Overwatch](https://kotaku.com/the-sound-of-a-hit-in-overwatch-is-made-by-beer-1778482754)
- Overwatch's hitmarker cross scales with the damage dealt (player report on Blizzard's forum; not a developer statement) **[community]** — [Blizzard forums: change on hitmarkers](https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/overwatch/t/undocumented-misleading-change-on-hitmarkers/15582)
- Team Fortress 2 (hit sounds added 6 Jan 2010) is the documented case of **pitch tied to damage**: `tf_dingaling_pitchmindmg` sets the pitch for a hit of 10 damage or less and `tf_dingaling_pitchmaxdmg` for 150 or more; critical hits add a higher-pitched sound; a separate **last-hit (kill) sound** plays when the damage kills; `tf_dingalingaling_repeat_delay` rate-limits the sound (0 = one per damage instance) **[wiki documenting Valve's cvars]** — [TF2 Wiki: Hit Sound](https://wiki.teamfortress.com/wiki/Hit_Sound)
- Apex Legends gives each state of the target its own sound: hitting a shield, breaking it, hitting an unshielded enemy, "then you get that satisfying finishing blow sound effect"; "it breaks the realism but it makes the game feel so much better to play" (Brad Snyder, Respawn, 2019). "My favorite sound in this game is the impact sound when you down an enemy" (Jordan Harmon, Respawn, 2019) **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- Hit sounds carry information, not just reward: Halo Infinite disables grenade hit sounds in ranked, and 343 logged it as a bug that Dynamo Grenade ticks still "act as audio hitmarkers" there (343, c. April 2022) **[primary]** — [Halo Infinite Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)
- Players are highly sensitive to the hit sound itself: Riot swapped Valorant's headshot sound during the 2020 beta and reverted it within about a day after complaints **[press]** — [Kotaku: Valorant's new headshot sound](https://kotaku.com/players-disliked-valorants-new-headshot-sound-so-much-t-1843183562)

Damage numbers
- Apex colours floating damage numbers by what was hit: the shield's tier colour while armour holds, red for flesh, gold/yellow for headshots, and the hit *sound* also changes between shield and health; the colours are covered by colour-blind modes. Guides disagree on details, so treat the exact scheme as approximate **[secondary]** — [Shacknews: Apex damage numbers](https://shacknews.com/article/110042/how-to-change-damage-numbers-in-apex-legends); [HyperX: reading Apex damage numbers](https://blog.hyperx.com/article/7152/how-to-read-damage-numbers-in-apex-legends); [EA: Apex accessibility features](https://careers.ea.com/able/resources/apex-legends/pc/features)

Impact effect on the target
- Valorant has separate head and body hit effects, in blood and in spark (no-blood) variants; when beta 0.50 accidentally disabled blood and the sparks were less distinct between head and body, hit-reg complaints spiked although few correctness bugs existed: "the visual feedback of the shot was presenting a misleading outcome to the player" (Kevin Lee, Riot, 6 Aug 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)
- Patch 1.07 (Sept 2020) fixes: (a) the server-confirmed hit effect now spawns **on the character at the body part hit and stays attached**, instead of at a fixed world position; (b) a new small **client-predicted spark** appears immediately at the bullet's world impact point, described as "an improved tracer" that "DOES NOT mean your shot has registered on the server"; (c) sparks were recoloured and reshaped to match blood so neither option reads better; (d) headshot effects were **scaled down** because at range they covered the head and made the target harder to track **[primary]** — [Valorant Patch Notes 1.07](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/game-updates/valorant-patch-notes-1-07/)
- Riot's warning about mixing predicted and confirmed effects: "The effects need to be clear in what they represent" (Lee, 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)

How late can confirmation be
- In Valorant, hit-confirm VFX and audio wait for the server, so they trail the tracer by the round trip plus processing: 40 ms each way gives about 80 ms; at 50 ms ping a shoulder hit's effect shows about 100 ms later, by which time a crouching target's head can be behind it. Riot classes this as a *clarity* failure of a *correct* system, and says "correctness bugs are much worse than clarity bugs" (Lee, 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)
- Attaching the confirmed effect to the character has its own cost: a shooter at 100 ms ping who hits a jiggle-peeker may never see the confirm because it follows the target behind cover (Lee, 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)
- CS2 (Nov 2024) added **Damage Prediction**: the client may play hit effects before the server confirms. Three toggles: body-shot effects (off by default), head-shot effects (off by default), kill ragdolls (on by default); it turns itself off at high or unstable ping; the cost is an occasional false hit or a "killed" enemy who is alive **[secondary reports of Valve's notes]** — [BLAST: damage prediction in CS2](https://blast.tv/cs/news/damage-prediction-in-cs2); [Waxpeer: what is damage prediction](https://waxpeer.com/blog/article/what-is-damage-prediction-in-cs2-and-should-you-use-it); [Refrag: November 2024 update](https://refrag.gg/blog/train-is-back-whats-new-in-the-november-13th-cs2-update)
- General threshold: a lecture deck on Swink's book gives 100 ms as the maximum response time for controls to feel comfortable, and about 240 ms as the average perceive-and-react cycle (after Card, Moran and Newell, 1983) **[secondary, academic slides]** — [Uni Bayreuth: GxD 07 Interfaces](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-07-Interfaces.pdf)
- A WPI study of CS:GO tested 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 ms of added local latency (its results section was not retrieved); the paper cites earlier work that local latency and target speed raise target-selection time, and a Claypool paper notes delay below 100 ms can be perceived (excerpt) **[academic]** — [Liu, Claypool et al. 2021](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~claypool/papers/csgo-net-local-21/paper.pdf)

Audio versus visual
- A 2024 KTH degree project had participants play a custom FPS with visual-only, audio-only and mixed hit feedback: most participants disliked the visual-only configuration, and the configurations made no significant difference to performance (excerpt; small student study, methods not checked) **[academic, low weight]** — [KTH degree project, 2024](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1881833/FULLTEXT01.pdf)
- A rule-of-thumb hitmarker spec from a small-studio guide: a 50–100 ms crosshair flash or X; a distinct visual and sound for headshots; a larger or different marker plus distinct audio for a kill (StraySpark, 2026) **[secondary]** — [StraySpark: FPS design fundamentals](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fps-game-design-fundamentals-ue5)

### Inferences
- The modern pattern is **two tiers**: something instant and local that says "your bullet went here" (tracer, predicted spark), and something authoritative that says "the server gave you this hit, on this body part" (marker, hit sound, attached effect, number). Each tier must look different enough that a player never mistakes the first for the second.
- On the evidence, a confirm that trails the shot by one round trip (about 40–100 ms) is accepted as normal by Riot, but *where* it is drawn matters more than *when*: draw it on the target's current body part, not at a stale world point.
- Valve's defaults suggest a ranking of what is worth predicting: the kill's visible result (ragdoll) first, per-hit effects only as an opt-in.
- In a long-TTK shield game a kill is many confirms, so the per-hit sound must be short, quiet enough to repeat 10+ times without fatigue, and rate-limited (TF2's repeat delay), with *escalation* reserved for state changes (shield break, headshot, kill).
- Checklist:
  - Reticle hitmarker on every confirmed hit; distinct variants for shield hit, health hit, headshot, kill.
  - Dedicated hit sound distinct from the gunshot, audible under sustained fire; variant per target state; optional pitch-by-damage.
  - Impact effect on the target that differs by surface (shield flare versus body) and by head/body, attached to the target, small enough not to hide the head.
  - Instant local impact cue (tracer end/spark) that is visibly *not* the confirm.
  - Confirm delay is no more than the round trip; nothing adds frames on top (queued audio, animation blend-in, UI tween delay).
  - Hit sounds through walls/grenades considered for ranked (information leak).

### Gaps
- **Overwatch's hit-sound pitch or volume scaling with damage, and its distinct elimination sound, were not confirmed** from a Blizzard source this session (*unverified recollection*; the GDC 2016 talk video is the place to check). The documented pitch-by-damage example is TF2.
- No first-party description was found for **Call of Duty's hitmarker** (shape, duration, sound, kill variant, armour-break sound) or **Destiny's damage numbers** (white versus yellow precision numbers), or **Halo Infinite's reticle hitmarkers**. A community recreation of Halo Infinite's markers lists a white hit marker lasting 0.5 s and a light-red kill marker lasting 0.4 s ([modworkshop mod page](https://modworkshop.net/mod/35468)); it is unofficial and should not be quoted as 343's numbers.
- No study was found that isolates the audio hit-tick against the visual marker with adequate power; the claim "audio matters more than visual" rests on developer practice and one small thesis.
- No developer statement was found on flinch/hit-reaction animation *on the target model* as shooter-facing feedback.

## 3. Shield-shooter specifics: reading shield state on the target and on yourself, and the pop

### Takeaway
In Halo-style combat the shield break is a rule change (precision headshots only count once the shield is gone), so the pop has to be unmistakable on sight and by ear, and the target's shield state has to be readable at a glance between hits. Halo Infinite's own launch-year reports are the best case study of this going wrong: enemy outlines washed out the shield effects, 343 conceded that "the shield FX could better communicate the opponent's shield state", and players called the per-hit flares too strong and too brief.

### Cited Findings
- The rule that makes the pop pivotal: in Halo, a Spartan's shield must be stripped before a precision headshot counts; on the BR75 "the first three shots inflict damage to the energy shield" and the headshot multiplier does not apply while the shield is up, so players are told to learn the visual effect of a "popped" shield **[press/secondary guides]** — [PC Gamer: How to not suck at Halo Infinite](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-to-not-suck-at-halo-infinite); [GamerGuides: BR75](https://www.gamerguides.com/halo-infinite/guide/weapons/unsc-weapons-human/br75-precision-rifle)
- What the pop looks and sounds like in Infinite: "a wave of color pulsing out from a spartan's body and a distinctive sound"; in Halo 3 the pop was "much more visible", with accent lines around the body; in the 2021 preview it was "difficult, maybe impossible, to look at an opponent and quickly tell if they have a shield or not" (PC Gamer, 2021) (excerpt) **[press]** — [PC Gamer: 8 things Halo Infinite needs to improve](https://www.pcgamer.com/8-things-halo-infinite-needs-to-improve-before-release)
- A beginner's guide describes "an electrical burst" on the target when the shield is depleted **[secondary]** — [KeenGamer: Halo Infinite multiplayer basics](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/guides/halo-infinite-multiplayer-basics-beginners-tips-and-tricks/)
- 343 after the technical preview (c. Sept 2021): outlines hurt shield and Overshield readability; "We've already made significant FX changes to better communicate the health of a target"; the goal is "You should always know if you're fighting a player who has an Overshield" (Sandbox Team) **[primary]** — [Technical Preview Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/technical-preview-outcomes)
- 343 after Season 1 (c. April 2022): players said shield flares are "too strong and too quick"; "We agree that the shield FX could better communicate the opponent's shield state"; Overshield state in combat is "on our radar as something we could better communicate"; Season 2 added outline opacity and thickness options **[primary]** — [Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)
- The shield's own sounds are treated as heritage and as information: 343 "decided to bring back all the original Master Chief shield sounds" on feedback (Fraser, 2021); the **third-person shield recharge sound** was then turned down because it revealed hiding players (2022) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite, March 2021](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021); [Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)
- Apex signals the same sequence on three channels: number colour (armour tier colour, then red), a different hit sound for shield and flesh, and a break sound, described by a guide as a "cracking sound" with the numbers turning red, and by Respawn's Brad Snyder as distinct sounds for shield hit, shield break, unshielded hit and finishing blow (2019) **[primary interview + secondary]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/); [CharlieIntel: health in Apex](https://www.charlieintel.com/apex-legends/what-is-the-amount-of-health-in-apex-legends-122140/)
- A player report shows how fragile the after-pop channel is: after a shield break some players could hear only the headshot flesh sound, not the body one **[community]** — [EA Forums: flesh hit sound not hearable](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/apex-legends-general-discussion-en/the-bullet-hitting-the-flesh-sound-is-not-hearable-/5453247)

### Inferences
- A long-TTK shield duel has three phases the shooter must be able to tell apart without reading a bar: shielded (flare per hit), popped (burst + sound, then a clearly different unshielded look), and recharging (visible/audible return). If the middle transition is weak, players keep body-shooting or commit to a melee/headshot too early, and the fight reads as random; Infinite's feedback history is exactly this complaint.
- Per-hit shield flare has two opposite failure modes, both reported against Infinite: too brief to read state between hits, and so bright it hides the target and the head.
- Team-colour outlines and shield effects compete for the same pixels at the silhouette edge; one must yield. 343 answered with outline opacity/thickness controls and reworked shield FX.
- Third-person shield sounds (recharge start, recharge loop) are tactical information for the enemy; their loudness is a balance knob, not just a mix choice.
- Checklist:
  - Per-hit shield flare on the target, localised to the hit, visible against the outline colour, not hiding the head.
  - Persistent cue of shield *level* on the target between hits (glow strength, colour shift, crackle at low shield).
  - Pop: a one-off burst visibly larger than a hit flare plus a unique sound, heard by the shooter at full priority at any range within the fight.
  - Unshielded look that persists until recharge (no shimmer; sparks; different hit effect and hit sound on flesh/armour).
  - Hitmarker and hit sound change at the pop; headshot-kill confirm distinct.
  - Own shield: bar, hit flash, low/empty alarm, recharge start and loop sounds; overshield has its own look on both sides.
  - Third-person recharge sound audible but not a beacon.

### Gaps
- **No developer talk or post was found that states the design rationale for the shield pop as the duel's pivot** (Bungie, 343, 1047 Games or Bungie's Destiny team); the reasoning above is assembled from guides and 343's feedback reports. The 343 support article "Shields in Halo Infinite" returned 403.
- Nothing was found on **Splitgate's** or **Destiny's** shield-break cues.
- *Unverified recollection:* classic Halo's player-side cues (a flashing bar with a repeating alarm at low/empty shield, a rising whine when recharge starts) are well known to players but were not confirmed from a source this session beyond 343 saying the classic shield sounds, including recharge, were brought back.
- No source gives timings (ms) or sizes for flare and pop effects.

## 4. Kill confirmation and reward: kill sound, marker, feed, medals, announcer, ragdoll

### Takeaway
Kills get their own sound on top of the hit sound in every game for which a source was found (TF2's last-hit sound, Apex's "finishing blow" and down impact), and Halo adds a second, slower layer of medals and announcer that 343 found players actively miss when it is absent. Valve's choice to predict kill ragdolls by default, while leaving per-hit effects unpredicted, indicates that the visible collapse of the target is the kill cue that most needs to be instant.

### Cited Findings
- TF2 has a separate, selectable **last-hit sound** that plays when your damage kills, extended to buildings in 2016 **[wiki documenting Valve's feature]** — [TF2 Wiki: Hit Sound](https://wiki.teamfortress.com/wiki/Hit_Sound)
- Apex: "then you get that satisfying finishing blow sound effect" (Snyder); the impact sound on downing an enemy is one audio designer's favourite sound in the game (Harmon) (2019) **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- Halo Infinite Big Team Battle shipped without the announcer calling medals; 343: "one thing that ultimately felt missing was the Medal feedback", so "Steitzer is in to call out your Medal accolades to keep the hype going" (c. April 2022) **[primary]** — [Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)
- Medal *presentation* matters: preview players liked callouts in the event feed but criticised medal size, placement and art; 343: "We agree that there is more work to be done here", moving to a more classic metallic design (c. Sept 2021). Players also asked for volume control over the announcer **[primary]** — [Technical Preview Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/technical-preview-outcomes)
- Valorant, as described by press: every kill plays a chord and the sound design is what makes an ace feel big (Edge review, 2020); skin lines' kill sounds rise in pitch or intensity with each successive kill in a round, tracked by the kill banner **[press/secondary; not Riot statements]** — [Edge review of Valorant (Magzter)](https://magzter.com/stories/Puzzle-Gaming/Edge/VALORANT); [Sportskeeda: skins with the best kill sounds](https://www.sportskeeda.com/valorant/5-valorant-skins-best-kill-sounds)
- CS2's Damage Prediction predicts **kill ragdolls by default** while head- and body-shot effects default off **[secondary]** — [Waxpeer: what is damage prediction](https://waxpeer.com/blog/article/what-is-damage-prediction-in-cs2-and-should-you-use-it)
- Doom (2016): glory kills grew out of "push forward combat" as a fast close-range finisher and are "only hundreds of milliseconds long" so the player keeps moving (Marty Stratton); enemies are "walking health and ammo packs", so the kill pays out a visible reward **[primary/press]** — [Bethesda: the guts and gore of Doom glory kills](https://bethesda.net/en-AU/news/the-guts-and-gore-of-doom-glory-kills); [Game Developer: the combat design of Doom](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-the-combat-design-of-i-doom-i-)
- Nijman's "33% chance of enemies exploding harmlessly on death" is an example of making the kill itself a spectacle (2013) **[press]** — [Game Developer: Vlambeer co-founder's advice](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)
- A blogger's framing of Vlambeer's talk: effects further from the player's direct action (hit animations, then deaths, corpses, lingering smoke) should communicate *more* clearly and can be bigger, because they happen less often than the shot itself (Craig Perko, 3 Jan 2014) **[secondary]** — [ProjectPerko: Feedback Scales](https://projectperko.blogspot.com/2014/01/feedback-scales.html)

### Inferences
- In a long-TTK game the kill is the pay-off for one to two seconds of sustained aim; if it is confirmed only by the target falling and a feed line, the fight "ends" without a full stop. The games above all add an unmistakable audio full stop and at least one on-screen confirmation near the reticle.
- Halo's medal + announcer layer works on a longer clock (hundreds of ms to seconds after the kill) and rewards *how* the kill happened; 343's BTB experience shows players notice its absence.
- Checklist:
  - Kill sound distinct from hit and shield-break sounds, never masked by the gun.
  - Kill marker at the reticle distinct from the hitmarker.
  - Target's death is visible at once (ragdoll/death animation starts on confirm; predicted if possible) with a readable impulse from the killing shot.
  - Kill feed line; own kills highlighted.
  - Medals for headshot, multi-kill, spree etc., sized and placed to be read without covering the fight; announcer on the bigger ones; announcer volume option.
  - Escalation across consecutive kills.

### Gaps
- No developer statement was found on **ragdoll/death-animation quality** as kill feedback, on **elimination banners**, or on Riot's own design intent for Valorant's kill sounds (Riot's audio posts found were general).
- *Unverified recollection:* Overwatch's elimination sound and CoD's red kill hitmarker are standard references but were not sourced this session.
- Nothing found from Bungie or 343 on the design of the multi-kill medal timing window.

## 5. Taking damage: direction, vignette, flinch, hit sound, low health, and learning who killed you

### Takeaway
The trend across Halo, Call of Duty and Apex is to remove or neuter flinch that moves the victim's aim and keep damage feedback as information only. For "who is shooting me", the strongest published practice is in the mix: 343, Respawn and Blizzard each built systems that make a gun aimed at the listener louder or harsher than one that is not. Developer sources link unreadable deaths with netcode complaints only indirectly, through Riot's clarity work and 343's desync reports.

### Cited Findings
- Halo Infinite's threat mix exists because of this exact complaint about Halo 5: "it sounds rich, but I often can't hear the enemy's weapon when they fire at me". The new system "detects all gun sounds frame by frame, and prioritizes them in a threat order to decide output sound volume for each gun"; previously volume was set "purely by distance", leaving "too many loud gun sounds constantly overwhelming a player" (Tojima, 2021) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite, March 2021](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)
- Apex: "The more a gun is pointing directly at you, the more threatening it feels" (filtering by the gun's facing) (Kraber, 2019) **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- Overwatch gives every other player an "importance" in the mix; enemies not engaging the listener "would receive less 'threat points' and be lower in the mix"; a path-length measure makes an enemy one floor up sound as near or far as the route to you; enemy sounds are muffled when you are in a safe hiding spot (Tomas Neumann, GDC 2016) **[press report of talk]** — [PCGamesN: Overwatch play by sound](https://pcgamesn.com/overwatch/overwatch-devs-on-creating-a-game-you-can-play-by-sound-and-announcing-dolby-atmos-support)
- Flinch, by game: Halo 5 removed flinch before release after its beta; one report says the flinch seen in the beta was not by design (2015) **[press]** — [Destructoid: flinch removed from Halo 5](https://www.destructoid.com/flinch-being-removed-from-halo-5/); [Sportskeeda: Halo 5 to remove much hated feature](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/halo-5-guardians-to-remove-much-hated-feature-in-the-final-release)
- Black Ops Cold War redesigned damage flinch "to work more as a feedback mechanism without significantly affecting aim" (Treyarch design lead Tony Flame, 2020); the side-effect players reported was stronger snipers **[press]** — [CharlieIntel: zero flinch on snipers](https://www.charlieintel.com/black-ops-cold-war/black-ops-cold-war-players-call-for-devs-to-address-overpowered-zero-flinch-on-snipers-98399/)
- Apex nearly eliminated aim flinch in Season 22 (2024); it had hit mouse players harder because they lack aim assist **[press]** — [Dexerto: Apex Season 22 aim flinch change](https://dexerto.com/apex-legends/apex-legends-players-ecstatic-over-season-22s-uncanny-aim-flinch-change-2847702)
- Destiny 2 kept flinch but made it a stat: from Season 17 (2022) Stability also reduces incoming flinch and each archetype has a base flinch resistance of 10–25%, with special weapons deliberately lower **[primary/press]** — [This Week at Bungie, 21 Apr 2022](https://www.bungie.net/7/en/News/article/51250); [MP1st: Season 17 changes](https://mp1st.com/news/destiny-2-season-17-changes-detailed-flinch-resistance-airborne-gunplay-more)
- Damage indicators have been studied: Young, Shahin and Nakane (2013) compared a red flash, a paper-doll and an x-ray damage indicator for their effect on the experience (exploratory; results not retrieved) **[academic]** — [U Manitoba HCI: embodied damage indicators](https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/projects-and-research/details/embodied-damage-indicators-in-fps)
- Killcams: Respawn's design director on why Apex has none: "we won't show you how you died until your whole squad is dead (otherwise you'll have cheaty info), at which point it's often not useful anymore"; a final-kill cam "costs memory on the server" (Jason McCord, 24 May 2021) **[press quoting developer]** — [Dexerto: Apex killcams](https://dexerto.com/apex-legends/apex-legends-dev-explains-why-they-wont-add-killcams-or-final-kill-replays-1581493)
- A journal article on kill cams argues they cause frustration when replays are inaccurate or intrusive; players in Overwatch and Apex forums report recaps that differ from what they saw and read that as lag, netcode or cheating **[academic + community]** — [Postphenomenology, kill-cams and shooters](https://www.ebsco.com/articles/sports-and-leisure/01f7fc96-3ca2-5cae-a6f8-81f861f50712/postphenomenology-kill-cams-and-shooters-exploring-the-code-of-replay-sequences); [EA Forums: kill cam reliability](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/apex-legends-general-discussion-en/kill-cam-reliability/5772412)
- Unreadable outcomes get filed as netcode: Halo Infinite players reported "dying behind walls", warping and inconsistent bullet and melee registration, which 343 grouped under desync (2022); Riot found most hit-reg reports were presentation, not registration (2020) **[primary]** — [Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report); [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)
- Melee is part of this in Halo: for Season 2 343 adjusted "melee rebound behavior", made the lunge "better snap towards your target" and reduced players "phasing" through each other so melee trades read consistently **[primary]** — [Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)
- A small-studio guide's mix priority: own weapon, then incoming damage, then enemy weapons, friendly weapons, abilities, with ambience and music ducked in combat (StraySpark, 2026) **[secondary]** — [StraySpark: FPS design fundamentals](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fps-game-design-fundamentals-ue5)

### Inferences
- Current competitive practice treats being shot as an *information* event (who, where, how much) and no longer as a handicap on the victim's aim; where flinch survives it is small and visual, or a build stat (Destiny).
- A death feels fair when the victim could have named the shooter's direction before dying. The cheapest lever is audio: guns aimed at you louder and less filtered than guns that are not.
- If the game has no killcam, the death screen must still answer "who, with what, from where" (killer's name, weapon, a look-at); if it has one, a replay that disagrees with what the victim saw is worse than none.
- Checklist:
  - Directional damage indicator per hit, per attacker, lasting long enough to turn toward.
  - Brief screen-edge flash on hit; distinct treatment for shield hit versus health hit versus own shield break.
  - Being-hit sound distinct for shield and for health; own shield break is loud and unique.
  - Incoming fire prioritised in the mix over distant and friendly fire.
  - No aim-moving flinch (or very small), consistent across inputs.
  - Low-shield/low-health state audible and visible without hiding the fight.
  - Death: killer, weapon and direction shown; kill feed; camera turns to killer or a replay.

### Gaps
- No developer or study figures were found for **directional indicator duration, vignette intensity, or low-health audio filtering/heartbeat**; a UX analysis of damage indicators by Jasper Stephenson exists ([Medium](https://medium.com/@jasper.stephenson/a-ux-analysis-of-first-person-shooter-damage-indicators-59ac9d41caf8)) but returned 403.
- No source directly tests the proposition "unreadable deaths are perceived as bad netcode"; it is an inference from Riot's and 343's reports.
- Nothing retrieved on Counter-Strike's aimpunch or on Halo Infinite's/CoD's death cams.

## 6. Enemy readability: silhouette and outline, animation, third-person fire, spatial audio, and effect noise

### Takeaway
Developers treat combat effects as a budget that competes with seeing the enemy: Riot shrank headshot effects because they hid the head, 343 cut sound density to make key combat sounds legible, and 343's outline system shows that a readability aid can itself erase another signal (shield state). Audio readability is engineered through threat-weighted mixing, per-character footsteps and occlusion that follows the path sound would travel.

### Cited Findings
- Halo Infinite's friend/foe **outlines** made red-versus-blue team colouring obsolete, which helped accessibility; preview feedback was that this hurt shield and Overshield readability and players wanted to turn outlines off; Season 2 added outline opacity and thickness options, with a full "off" option acknowledged as philosophically fine but slower to build (343, 2021–22) **[primary + press]** — [Technical Preview Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/technical-preview-outcomes); [Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report); [Pure Xbox: technical preview feedback](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2021/09/343_industries_reveals_the_key_feedback_from_halo_infinites_technical_preview)
- Valorant highlights enemies with an outline whose default is red, with four selectable colours including ones for colour-vision deficiency **[secondary guides]** — [TheSpike: changing enemy colour in Valorant](https://www.thespike.gg/valorant/tips/how-to-change-enemy-color-in-valorant)
- Riot's engineering post "VALORANT Shaders and Gameplay Clarity" (Brandon Wang) frames shaders as a balance of art style, performance and game integrity, with readability considerations in material response and in what is cut at low quality (excerpt; body not retrieved) **[primary]** — [Riot Technology: VALORANT Shaders and Gameplay Clarity](https://technology.riotgames.com/node/110)
- Effects that hide the target are cut back: Valorant scaled down headshot effects because at long range they covered the head and made the player harder to track (Patch 1.07, 2020) **[primary]** — [Valorant Patch Notes 1.07](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/game-updates/valorant-patch-notes-1-07/)
- Overwatch's audio pillars: a clean mix, sounds that can be pinpointed, sounds that carry gameplay information, and descriptive hero voice lines; every hero has a unique footstep so players can tell who is coming round a corner (Scott Lawlor and Tomas Neumann, "The Elusive Goal: Play by Sound", GDC 2016) **[primary listing + press]** — [GDC Vault: Overwatch, The Elusive Goal: Play by Sound](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023010/Overwatch-The-Elusive-Goal-Play); [PCGamesN: Overwatch play by sound](https://pcgamesn.com/overwatch/overwatch-devs-on-creating-a-game-you-can-play-by-sound-and-announcing-dolby-atmos-support)
- Apex plays unique footstep assets "for when a character is a floor above you" (and below, and on roofs); the team "had to keep pushing and pulling up and down decibels" between gunfire and footsteps; in Titanfall multiplayer Foley was "hardly ever heard over the bombast" (Kraber, 2019) **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- Apex carves room for combat cues: a looping ability sound (Stim) has its low and high end removed so it does not mask combat sounds; the Ring's loop drops in level when the player is not looking at it; ambience avoids "false positives" that could be mistaken for an enemy (Snyder, Parsons, 2019) **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect: Apex Legends sound](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- Halo Infinite's acoustics system "simulates the way sound travels through the environment, reflecting off the walls" and decides "how much to filter (i.e. muffle) the sound" by path, using obstruction, occlusion, propagation and reverberation (Chase Thompson, 2021) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite, March 2021](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)
- Mis-set headshot/hit effects are read as wrong outcomes: a body-hit effect left in the world where a crouching head then arrives looks like a headshot that did no headshot damage (Lee, 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)

### Inferences
- Every effect drawn on or near an enemy (shield flare, hit sparks, outline, muzzle flash, damage numbers) should be checked against one question: can the shooter still see the head and the direction of travel during and after it.
- Readability aids should be tested together, not one at a time; Infinite's outline was fine alone and harmful to the shield signal.
- For a game with its own mixer, threat weighting (who is aiming at me) and a per-source cap on simultaneous gun voices are the documented ways to keep an 8v8 with vehicles legible.
- Checklist:
  - Enemy silhouette separable from every map background (team colour or outline), with outline strength adjustable and not masking shield effects.
  - Head readable through hit and shield effects at mid and long range.
  - Third-person muzzle flash and tracer visible from the victim's side and pointing back to the shooter.
  - Enemy footsteps audible and louder/more distinct than friendly ones; vertical position distinguishable.
  - Gunfire spatialised with distance layers and occlusion; guns aimed at the listener prioritised.
  - Effect density capped in large fights; long loops ducked or band-limited.

### Gaps
- **Riot's "combat clarity" as a named design pillar was not found in a Riot post** this session; the phrase appears to be a paraphrase of Riot's clarity work (hit-reg clarity, shaders/gameplay clarity). The body of the shaders post was not retrieved.
- No source retrieved on Overwatch 2's effect-opacity rules, on Apex's enemy highlighting, or on animation readability of strafes and jumps.
- *Unverified recollection:* Overwatch mixes enemy footsteps louder than allies' and gives enemy ultimates different voice lines from friendly ones; not confirmed from a source here.

## 7. Timing and animation principles applied to guns

### Takeaway
The documented timing rules are few but firm: local response on the next frame and never delayed for agreement with the server; server-confirmed feedback one round trip later; roughly 100 ms as the ceiling for a response to feel direct. Hit-stop is a melee technique that first-person games use sparingly because freezing or shaking a first-person camera disorients; small cadence numbers (a 0.2 s change to Halo 3's sniper) are known to change how a weapon plays.

### Cited Findings
- Response starts one frame after input (flash, tracer); the bullet is never delayed (Lee, Riot, 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)
- Swink (2007): game feel is "the tactile sensation of manipulating a digital agent", "mostly subconscious, a combination of sights, sounds, and instant response to action"; polish (particles, screen shake, view-angle shifts, squash and stretch) exists to "convey the physical properties of objects through their motion and interaction"; "A little screen shake or spray of particles can make all the difference in the world to a game's feel"; if feel is "off by just a little bit, a game's goose is cooked" **[primary, author's article]** — [Swink: Game Feel, The Secret Ingredient (Gamasutra, 23 Nov 2007)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-feel-the-secret-ingredient)
- Thresholds taught from Swink's book: response within 100 ms to feel comfortable; about 240 ms for a full perceive-and-react cycle (Card, Moran and Newell) **[secondary slides]** — [Uni Bayreuth: GxD 07 Interfaces](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/GxD-07-Interfaces.pdf)
- The shot cycle as a chain to audit: input, fire animation, muzzle flash and sound, recoil, trace, hit detection, hit feedback, recoil recovery, ready; "Every step must feel instantaneous and connected" (StraySpark, 2026) **[secondary]** — [StraySpark: FPS design fundamentals](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fps-game-design-fundamentals-ue5)
- Cadence is feel: Jaime Griesemer's GDC 2010 talk is about changing one of the sniper rifle's 200+ fields, the minimum time between shots, from 0.5 s to 0.7 s, because the rifle was too effective at close range and on body shots; cutting the magazine or lengthening reload or zoom time were rejected as fixing only part of it **[primary listing + press]** — [GDC Vault: Design in Detail](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012211); [Game Developer: Halo 3's sniper rifle by 0.2 seconds](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-how-changing-i-halo-3-i-s-sniper-rifle-fire-rate-by-0-2-seconds-changed-the-game)
- Hit-stop in first person: Obsidian uses hitstop on first-person melee in Avowed but found camera shake too disorienting and moved shake to the HUD **[press]** — [PC Gamer: Avowed hitstop](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/if-youve-been-worried-about-avowed-not-looking-punchy-enough-obsidians-combat-designer-has-a-hitstop-animation-superpower-that-might-reassure-you)
- Hit-stop has detractors even on melee in a shooter: Space Marine 2 players asked for a setting to disable the fraction-of-a-second melee/parry pause **[community]** — [Focus forums: disable hit stop](https://community.focus-entmt.com/focus-entertainment/space-marine-2/ideas/7565-add-in-game-setting-to-disable-hit-stop-effect)
- 2D reference values for hit-stop (not FPS): 4–8 frames on a sword hit, 6–10 frames for heavy weapons at 60 fps **[secondary, low authority]** — [FreeGameSprites: 2D combat juice](https://freegamesprites.com/es/news/game-feel-2d-combat-juice-tricks)
- Finishers must be short: Doom's glory kills are "only hundreds of milliseconds long" (Stratton) **[primary]** — [Bethesda: glory kills](https://bethesda.net/en-AU/news/the-guts-and-gore-of-doom-glory-kills)
- Weapon handling animations are designed not to take the player off target: MW2019's aim-down-sights reload was added because otherwise the weapon "would go off to the side, and you'd have to retrain to get to your target" (Grigsby, 2019) **[press]** — [GamesRadar: MW animation director](https://www.gamesradar.com/call-of-duty-modern-warfare-animation-director-details-the-reboots-slick-gun-animations)
- Melee recovery and lunge consistency were tuned as a feel fix in Halo Infinite Season 2 (rebound behaviour, lunge snap) **[primary]** — [Season 1 Outcomes Report](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)

### Inferences
- No evidence was found that any major competitive FPS applies global hit-stop to gunfire. The equivalents used are per-object: a held bright frame on the muzzle flash, a hit flash/flare on the target, a short kick with eased recovery, and sound transients.
- "Anticipation" is close to zero for firing (the response must be immediate); "impact" is the first frame's flash and transient; "follow-through" is recovery, tail, shell and smoke. Sluggishness normally hides in the follow-through blocking the next input: long draw, reload or post-melee lockouts.
- Audit by frame-stepping a capture: count frames from click to flash, to sound onset, to kick peak, to hitmarker, to hit sound, to target effect.

### Gaps
- **No published draw/ready/swap times or animation-cancel rules as "feel" guidance** were found from a developer (the tuning-numbers researchers may have the values); no source on "first frame of response" budgets beyond Riot's.
- Whether Destiny or Halo applies a brief hit-stop on melee was not confirmed.
- Swink's book text was not consulted directly; the 100 ms / 240 ms figures come from a lecture deck about it. One summary's stronger claim that "above 240 ms real-time control is broken" could not be verified.

## 8. Canonical sources and what each actually says

### Takeaway
Of the canonical references, the ones that give checkable, shooter-specific practice are Riot's hit-registration post and Patch 1.07 notes, 343's Inside Infinite audio post and its two outcome reports, Respawn's 2019 audio interview and Blizzard's "Play by Sound" talk. Swink, Nijman and Jonasson/Purho give principles and a vocabulary, not shooter parameters, and the academic work on "juice" is mixed about whether it changes anything measurable.

### Cited Findings
- **Steve Swink, "Game Feel" (article 2007; book 2008).** Feel is real-time control of virtual objects in a simulated space, with interactions emphasised by polish; polish conveys physical properties **[primary + reference]** — [Swink 2007](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-feel-the-secret-ingredient); [Wikipedia: Game feel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_feel)
- **Jan Willem Nijman (Vlambeer), "The Art of Screenshake" (Control Conference, Nov 2013, ~45 min).** Takes a dull side-scrolling shooter and applies about 30 small tweaks, each making it a bit better; confirmed examples: shake opposite to fire direction; a 33% chance that enemies explode harmlessly on death **[press]** — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/vlambeer-co-founder-shares-advice-on-building-better-action-games)
- A contemporaneous reading of that talk: Vlambeer's feedback is "often immediate but also often prolonged"; some of it (pauses, strengthened bass) conveys no information and exists to "hammer it in" (Perko, 2014) **[secondary]** — [ProjectPerko: Feedback Scales](https://projectperko.blogspot.com/2014/01/feedback-scales.html)
- **Jaime Griesemer, "Design in Detail: Changing the Time Between Shots for the Sniper Rifle from 0.5 to 0.7 Seconds for Halo 3" (GDC 2010).** A balance talk, not a feedback talk: Halo balances 1000+ objects, 40+ weapons, 200+ fields per weapon; one cadence field fixed a role problem **[primary listing]** — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012211)
- **David Helsby (Bungie), "The Art of First Person Animation for Destiny" (GDC 2015).** First-person animation should feel responsive and physical while supporting immersion; camera work and avoiding motion sickness are central **[primary listing + press]** — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022297/The-Art-of-First-Person); [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/destiny-smooth-fps-boxing/)
- **Kurt Loudy and Jake Campbell (id), "Embracing Push Forward Combat in DOOM" (GDC 2018).** "Push forward combat" is a filter for whether a mechanic belongs; pillars are fast movement, unique weapons, strong AI archetypes and the player's strength; regenerating health, reloading and cover were dropped so the player takes what they need from enemies **[primary listing + press]** — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024940/Embracing-Push-Forward-Combat-in); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-the-combat-design-of-i-doom-i-)
- **Respawn audio team, A Sound Effect interview (17 Apr 2019).** Four-plus distance layers per gun, facing-dependent filtering, distinct sounds for shield hit, break, flesh and finishing blow, feedback preferred over realism **[primary interview]** — [A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)
- **Scott Lawlor and Tomas Neumann (Blizzard), "Overwatch: The Elusive Goal: Play by Sound" (GDC 2016).** The director's brief was a game you can "play by sound"; pillars and the importance/threat mix as above **[primary listing + press]** — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023010/Overwatch-The-Elusive-Goal-Play); [Game Developer video write-up](https://gamedeveloper.com/audio/video-how-i-overwatch-i-was-designed-so-people-could-play-by-sound-)
- **Riot (Kevin Lee), "The State of Hit Registration" (6 Aug 2020) and Patch 1.07.** Correctness versus clarity; predicted versus confirmed feedback; attach confirms to the character **[primary]** — [Riot dev post](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/); [Patch 1.07](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/game-updates/valorant-patch-notes-1-07/)
- **DICE (Anders Clerwall), HDR audio (GDC 2009).** Loudness-window mixing so the loudest, most important sounds dominate **[primary]** — [Frostbite](https://www.ea.com/frostbite/news/how-hdr-audio-makes-battlefield-bad-company-go-boom)
- **343 Industries, Inside Infinite (March 2021) and the outcome reports (2021, 2022).** Threat-ordered gun mix, fewer denser sounds, shield FX and outline readability, medal feedback **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021); [Technical Preview Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/technical-preview-outcomes); [Season 1 Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)
- **Academic work on juiciness.** Hicks, Gerling, Dickinson and Vanden Abeele, "Juicy Game Design: Understanding the Impact of Visual Embellishments on Player Experience" (CHI PLAY 2019, pp. 185–197): two studies (40 and 32 participants) with two research games, one an FPS ("Dungeon Descent"); outcomes not retrieved **[academic]** — [CHI PLAY 2019 record](https://bibtex.github.io/CHI-PLAY-2019-HicksGDA.html)
- Juul and Begy's 46-participant study found no significant difference between juicy and non-juicy versions of a tile-matching game, and used no standard player-experience measures (excerpt, as summarised in later juiciness literature returned by search; which of those papers carries the summary was not pinned) **[academic, via later paper]** — [Hicks et al. 2024, Juicy Audio (Staffordshire repository)](https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/8527)
- Hicks, Rogers, Gerling and Nacke (CHI PLAY 2024) argue from interviews with audio designers that juicy-design principles also apply to sound **[academic]** — [Juicy Audio](https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/8527)
- WPI (Claypool's group): local latency and target speed both raise target-selection time; a 38-person custom-FPS study shows QoE degrading with latency (FDG 2024) **[academic]** — [Liu, Claypool et al. 2021](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~claypool/papers/csgo-net-local-21/paper.pdf); [Adaptive time delay, FDG 2024](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~claypool/papers/adaptive-fdg-24/paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The craft literature is strong on *what to add* and weak on *numbers*; the numbers that exist are latency relationships (Riot), layer counts (Respawn, 343) and cvar ranges (TF2). An audit should therefore be a presence/absence and ordering audit first, and a tuning exercise second.
- The academic record does not show that juice improves performance; its support is for experience and preference, and even that is mixed. The practical argument for feedback in a competitive shooter is information and clarity (Riot, 343), not decoration.

### Gaps
- **Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho, "Juice it or lose it" (2012):** no source could be retrieved this session. *Unverified recollection:* a Breakout clone is made progressively "juicier" with tweening/easing, squash and stretch, sound, particles and screen shake, with no rule changes.
- The GDC Vault videos themselves (Lawlor/Neumann, Loudy/Campbell, Helsby, Griesemer) were not watched; content is from listings and press summaries.
- Game Developer's 2019 write-up of Griesemer's talk calls the change a reload change; the GDC title says time between shots, so the write-up looks mistaken.
- No Bungie written piece on Destiny gun feel/audio and no Infinity Ward or Sledgehammer piece on shot feedback was retrieved; nothing on Game Maker's Toolkit or Errant Signal analyses.

## 9. The most commonly cited reasons an early-stage FPS's combat "feels off" despite correct numbers

### Takeaway
Across the sources found, the recurring diagnoses are: weak or mis-layered gun and hit audio; hits that are not confirmed clearly on more than one channel; feedback that is late, misplaced or contradicts the outcome; and animation or camera behaviour that either ignores the action or fights the aim. I found no survey that counts how often each is cited, so the ranking below is my ordering by weight of evidence, not a measured frequency.

### Cited Findings
- "A weak or inappropriate sound effect can completely ruin the impact of a weapon" **[secondary]** — [Wayline: weapon feel in indie games](https://www.wayline.io/blog/weapon-feel-indie-games)
- A weak-sounding weapon "usually has a missing layer or layers fighting each other" (thin = transient without body; muffled = body without transient) **[secondary]** — [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-weapon-sounds/)
- Hits "without graphic or sound effects to confirm them feel a lot more ambiguous" (player feedback on an indie build) **[community]** — [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/13379652)
- Misleading (not missing) feedback generates "bad hit reg" reports even when registration is correct (Riot, 2020) **[primary]** — [The State of Hit Registration](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)
- Any delay in the chain input → animation → flash/sound → recoil → hit feedback "breaks the feedback loop" **[secondary]** — [StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fps-game-design-fundamentals-ue5)
- Weapon reaction to the player's own actions is "commonly overlooked" (H1Z1, 2014) **[press]** — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-09-29-how-feel-trumps-realism-in-h1z1s-weapon-design.html)
- Too much sound is as bad as too little: Halo 5 "sounds rich, but I often can't hear the enemy's weapon when they fire at me" (343, 2021) **[primary]** — [Inside Infinite](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)
- Unclear target state: "the shield FX could better communicate the opponent's shield state" (343, 2022) **[primary]** — [Season 1 Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)
- Camera effects that move the aim or the screen centre draw complaints of disorientation and sickness (BF2042, Avowed) **[community + press]** — [EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/battlefield-2042-general-discussion-en/revert-visual-recoil---motion-sickness/7184171/replies/7184199); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/if-youve-been-worried-about-avowed-not-looking-punchy-enough-obsidians-combat-designer-has-a-hitstop-animation-superpower-that-might-reassure-you)
- Flinch that moves the victim's aim is widely disliked and has been removed or neutered in Halo 5, Cold War and Apex **[press]** — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/flinch-being-removed-from-halo-5/); [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/apex-legends/apex-legends-players-ecstatic-over-season-22s-uncanny-aim-flinch-change-2847702)
- Missing reward layer: "one thing that ultimately felt missing was the Medal feedback" (343, 2022) **[primary]** — [Season 1 Outcomes](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)

### Inferences
- Ranking by weight of evidence in this research (strongest first):
  1. Gun audio lacks punch or separation: missing transient/low-end thud, no tail, own gun not above the rest, bursts smeared.
  2. Hit confirmation is single-channel, too subtle, or identical for every outcome (no difference between shield hit, flesh hit, headshot, break, kill).
  3. Feedback is late or in the wrong place: confirms delayed beyond the round trip, effects left at stale positions, predicted and confirmed cues indistinguishable.
  4. Target state is unreadable: shield level, the pop, and the unshielded state do not look or sound different enough; outlines or effects hide them.
  5. The kill has no full stop: no kill sound/marker, slow or limp death response, no medal/announcer layer.
  6. Incoming damage is uninformative: no clear direction, incoming fire buried in the mix, deaths with no visible cause (read as netcode).
  7. Camera and viewmodel misbehave: shake or visual recoil that moves the aim, linear recoveries, a viewmodel that does not react, handling animations that block input or pull the gun off target.
  8. Visual and audio clutter: too many simultaneous effects and sounds, so the important ones do not stand out.
- For a long-TTK shield shooter, items 2 and 4 carry more weight than in a short-TTK game, because the player must read a continuous state over one to two seconds instead of a single hit-or-miss event.

### Gaps
- **No frequency-ranked survey** of r/gamedev, r/truegaming or post-mortems was found; the ranking is judgement.
- No Game Maker's Toolkit or comparable video-essay text summary was retrieved.
- Indie-specific sources found were low-authority blogs and forum comments; no post-mortem from a shipped indie arena shooter (e.g. Splitgate, Diabotical) on fixing combat feel was found.
