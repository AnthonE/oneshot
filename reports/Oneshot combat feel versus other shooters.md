# Oneshot copied Halo's counts, not its help

Oneshot's combat most likely feels off because it reproduces Halo 2's kill times and target speed without the aim help Halo pairs them with, and then reports hits through feedback that is thin and partly misleading. The Battle Rifle needs 11 of 12 rounds, the last on the head, for a 1.29 s kill on a target that strafes at 6.10 m/s and reverses direction in 234 ms on Quake III's acceleration constants, while bullet magnetism is compiled in but switched off; Halo 2's rifle bends near misses inside a 3° cone out to about 52 m. Shooters built for unassisted mouse aim kill with their default rifle in roughly 0.7 to 1.1 s, not 1.3 to 2.5 s. Feedback is the second cluster: every non-kill hit shows the same white X, the player's own round sparks on the wall behind the enemy it hit, and a headshot on shields sounds like a body shot. Third, the sandbox contradicts itself: the spawn Magnum kills in 0.72 s against the rifle's 1.29 s, a melee removes 35% of a body where Halo's removes 61 to 65%, and a frag cannot break a full shield. The netcode is, on paper, level with or ahead of the genre's best and is the least likely cause. None of this was playtested or measured in live matches: oneshot's numbers are read from its code and its own perfect-aim range test, the reference values come from developers, game-file transcriptions and community measurements, and the link between the two is inference that a handful of cheap experiments would confirm or kill.

## Eight candidate causes, ranked by evidence and none of them playtested

Three kinds of statement appear below, and they are kept apart. A statement followed by a `path:line` reference was **read from oneshot's code** as audited at commit `1778766` on 2026-11-09; sim files (`consts.rs`, `arms.rs`, `world.rs`, `movement.rs`, `bot.rs`) live in `crates/sim/src/`. A statement followed by a link is **sourced from another game**: a developer post, a transcription of the game's own data files (Halopedia's tag pages, id's Quake III source), or a community measurement, which is named as such. Anything described as derived, arithmetic or inferred is **inference**: either sums on top of those two, or a judgement that a difference matters to feel. The only thing executed was the repo's own range test, which fires a perfect shooter at a stationary target (`crates/sim/tests/range.rs:1-5`). No match was played, no capture was taken, and the repo holds no accuracy, kill-time, rewind-depth or latency records from real matches, so every ranking below is a ranking of evidence, not of measured effect.

One piece of context sits outside the ranking. The whole combat model is five to seven days old and moved in several passes toward "Halo 2's numbers" between 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-09. By the audit's reading of the git log, magnetism entered the sim around 4 to 5 November and was switched off on the 7th (commit `119105f`, "off for now"), bots were weakened on the 8th (`5cfd793`), and the camera's kick on firing and flinch on being hit were removed on the 9th (`97b9606`). If the operator can date when combat began to feel off, that date separates rank 1 from rank 2 more cheaply than any experiment.

## A 1.29-second kill, a Quake strafe and no bent bullets

On everything Halo prints on a stat card, the port is faithful, and that is the trap. Oneshot's guns are declared "tuned to Halo 2's feel: its counts … and times carried onto our 100 shield and 100 health" (`arms.rs:1-8`). The Battle Rifle (BR) deals 10 a round in three-round bursts on a 406 ms cycle, so ten rounds strip the shield and the eleventh, in the head, kills: **four bursts and 1.29 s**, seven bursts and 2.51 s to the body alone (`arms.rs:330-350`; `crates/sim/tests/range.rs:277-287`). Halo 2's BR is 6 a round against a 70 shield and 45 health, also four bursts, about 1.33 s by the tags' arithmetic and 1.45 s by a community stopwatch ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/H2:Battle_Rifle), [Bungie.net forum](https://www.bungie.net/en/Forums/Post/2326524?page=0&path=0)). The shield waits 5.0 s and refills in 1.67 s against Halo 2's 5 s and 2 s (`consts.rs:127-131`; [Halopedia](https://halopedia.org/Spartan_(multiplayer))). Run and strafe speeds of 6.86 and 6.10 m/s are Halo's 2.25 and 2.0 world units a second at 3.048 m per unit (`consts.rs:44-52`; [Halo Editing Kit tutorial](https://nikon.bungie.org/misc/hek_tutorial/references/general/halo_player_stats.html)), and the high, 1.40 s jump is Halo's too (`consts.rs:63-68`). The time-to-kill (TTK) on paper is Halo 2's to within 50 ms.

What the stat card omits is what oneshot lacks. Bullet magnetism, which bends a round that would narrowly miss onto the target, exists in the sim but is off: `pub const MAGNETISM: bool = false`, with the comment "a round goes where the cone sends it" (`consts.rs:103-107`, gated at `world.rs:1162`). No other aim help exists anywhere in the client, room or server, and a gamepad drives menus only (`crates/client/src/pad.rs:1-15`). Halo 2's tags, as Halopedia transcribes them, give the BR an auto-aim cone of **3° out to 17 world units (about 52 m)**, the Magnum 3° to 12, the SMG 6° to 12, plus a separate, controller-only reticle friction ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/H2:Battle_Rifle), [Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/H2:Magnum), [Halopedia](https://halopedia.org/Magnetism)). Halopedia records that the bullet-bending half applies to mouse and keyboard as well ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Auto-aim)), and even with it, a Halo Tracker sample put Halo Infinite's top-100 controller players 16.4% ahead of top-100 mouse players on accuracy ([MeinMMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/fan-proves-as-controller-players-you-have-a-huge-advantage-in-halo-infinite,756017)). Oneshot's dormant per-gun values are 0.3° to 0.4° (`arms.rs:317-318`, `332-333`, `353-354`). By my geometry that widens a body at 20 m by 0.14 m a side where a 3° cone spans about 1 m; the two mechanisms are not identical, so read that as scale, not equivalence. The inference all three research strands reached independently is that a mouse-played game with Halo's counts and zero magnetism is harsher than any official Halo on PC.

The target, meanwhile, dodges like Quake. Oneshot's ground model is "Quake's model on the ground … at Halo 2's speeds and jump" (`consts.rs:44-48`), with `ACCEL = 10.0` and `FRICTION = 6.0` (`consts.rs:55-56`): the same values as Quake III's `pm_accelerate 10` and `pm_friction 6` ([id Software source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/id-Software/Quake-III-Arena/master/code/game/bg_pmove.c)). A re-run of the movement step gives a strafer **zero velocity 78 ms after reversing and full opposite speed at 234 ms** (derived from `movement.rs:61-83`, `146-154`; not printed by any test), which matches the 0.08 s and 0.23 s derived from Quake III's source. Nothing in the firing code reads the shooter's motion, so strafing, jumping and crouching cost no accuracy (`world.rs:881-890`). Halo's own acceleration is not published anywhere the research could find. The evidence that it matters is indirect but first-party: 343 raised strafe acceleration after Halo 5's beta ([MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/heres-beta-feedback-helped-improve-halo-5-guardians)), and cut Halo Infinite's strafe and forward acceleration scalars from 1 to 0.78 in February 2025 after years of complaints about its instant strafe ([Halo Support](https://support.halowaypoint.com/hc/en-us/articles/34335369079700-Halo-Infinite-Spring-Update-2025-Patch-Notes), seen through a search summary). Tactical shooters go the other way entirely: a CS2 counter-strafe takes about 110 to 125 ms to stop and the rifle is inaccurate above roughly a third of top speed ([TurboSmurfs](https://turbosmurfs.gg/?p=8949), community timing seen through a search summary).

Put together, the geometry is unforgiving. A body is a 0.70 m wide cylinder with a 0.44 m head (`consts.rs:97-102`). The rest of this paragraph is arithmetic on those constants, not data. A full-speed strafer crosses its own width in 115 ms and one BR burst lasts 141 ms, so an untracked burst cannot land whole. The four-burst kill tolerates one missed round in twelve. Each burst that fails adds a 406 ms cycle: if six rounds in ten land, the ten shield-stripping hits alone take six bursts and the kill passes 2.0 s. There is also a scaling mismatch. Speeds were converted from Halo at full scale, but the Editing Kit gives Halo's player as 0.298 world units wide and 0.70 tall, which is 0.91 m by 2.14 m at the same conversion ([Halo Editing Kit tutorial](https://nikon.bungie.org/misc/hek_tutorial/references/general/halo_player_stats.html)). Oneshot's body is therefore about 23% narrower and moves about 8.7 body-widths a second against Halo's 6.7. This is an inference from collision dimensions, since no Halo hitbox figures were found, and it cuts both ways: oneshot's head is larger than the 0.094-unit (0.29 m) helmet Bungie quoted for Halo 3 ([Bungie Weekly Update](https://halo.bungie.org/bwu/index.html?item=182)).

Other shooters show what a mouse can sustain. The games surveyed split into a short-kill cluster that penalises movement and a long-kill cluster of roughly 0.7 to 1.1 s for the default rifle, and every long-kill game buys the shooter a way to hit: Apex halves the strafe speed of a player aiming an R-301 ([Apex wiki](https://apexlegends.wiki.gg/wiki/R-301_Carbine)), Overwatch 2 enlarged projectiles by 0.05 to 0.15 m in the same patch that raised health ([Blizzard](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/patch-notes/live/2024/02)), and Splitgate, the one commercial Halo-like led by mouse players, gave PC no aim assist ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/126314/how-does-aim-assist-work-in-splitgate)) but dropped the shield layer for 100 regenerating health and now sits at 0.74 to 0.91 s for its assault rifles ([Splitgate wiki](https://splitgatearenareloaded.wiki.gg/wiki/Health), [Splitgate wiki](https://splitgatearenareloaded.wiki.gg/wiki/Trailblazer)). Respawn lowered Apex's TTK in 2025 because the longer one made players "value mobility too much over tactical positioning" ([EA](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/apex-legends/news/28-0-designers-notes)). Where pads and mice share lobbies, Apex and Call of Duty both cut controller aim assist after finding it too strong at close range ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/apex-legends/aim-assist-nerf-season-22), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/call-of-duty/controller-players-are-winning-slightly-more-in-close-range-engagements-treyarch-throws-a-live-grenade-into-controller-vs-mouse-debate-says-aim-assist-will-be-less-forgiving-in-black-ops-7), both seen through search summaries), which is the range where an unassisted mouse has the most tracking to do. Oneshot's rifle sits above the 0.7 to 1.1 s band at perfect aim, and its body-only time is longer than anything in the table.

Three experiments settle rank 1. First, set `MAGNETISM` to true and play the same map both ways, then sweep `Arm::magnet` from today's 0.4° toward Halo's 3°; the covering test already passes either way (`crates/sim/tests/combat.rs:683-705`), and the determinism golden moves with it. Second, count from the replays the room already records: rounds fired and landed per gun, head share, and time from first hit to kill. By my thresholds, a realised BR kill near 1.3 to 1.6 s refutes the hypothesis and one past 2 s confirms it. Third, frame-count a strafe reversal in Halo 2 itself and compare it with 234 ms, as Splitgate's developers reportedly did for basic movement timing ([Last Minute Continue](https://lastminutecontinue.com/review/hands-on-splitgate/), search summary). If Halo's is slower, scale `ACCEL` down in one step of 10% or more, the minimum change Bungie's Jaime Griesemer says players notice ([attendee notes on his GDC 2010 talk](https://gamrconnect.vgchartz.com/post.php?id=3259176)). The alternative to restoring Halo's help is Splitgate's route: a rifle kill nearer 0.8 to 1.0 s.

## One white X for every hit, and sparks on the wall behind the target

A Halo-style duel turns on one instant, the shield break, because precision headshots only kill once the shield is gone (`world.rs:2464`). Oneshot marks that instant weakly for the shooter. The hitmarker has two states: a 26-pixel white X shown for 0.22 s for every non-kill hit, whether shield, health, headshot or shield break, and a red one for a kill (`crates/client/src/hud.rs:45-51`, `1180-1186`, `1467-1491`). The hit sound does vary, but a head hit absorbed by shields plays the shield-hit sound at gain 0.35, so nothing tells the player their aim is on the head until the shield is already down (`crates/client/src/sound/mod.rs:1250-1257`). The reticle is the same static four strokes for every gun (`crates/client/src/hud.rs:883-905`). Damage numbers, which the design names (`docs/DESIGN.md:154-157`), are absent, and the kill cam the design lists as done is computed in the netcode but never drawn (`crates/client-net/src/core.rs:2590-2662`; `crates/client/src/lib.rs:2073`). The reward layer after a kill is not the gap: a kill sound, a red kill marker, a kill feed, 43 medals and a voiced announcer all exist (`crates/client/src/hud.rs:46-51`, `1255-1269`; `crates/proto/src/medal.rs:151-204`).

Worse than thin, some of it points the wrong way. The client predicts its own shot as a ray against the map only, straight down the reticle, and throws away the server's own-shot event that carries the round's true end (`crates/client-net/src/core.rs:1318-1332`, `1633-1634`). So when a round hits an enemy, the immediate world-space effect is a bullet mark on the wall behind them (`crates/client/src/look/fx/shots.rs:112-117`), and no impact is drawn where the round stopped. The target's shield flare is driven by the drawn shield value dropping, so it appears at least one interpolation delay (31 ms or more) after the hitmarker (`crates/client/src/look/fx/bodies.rs:182-195`). Confirmation therefore arrives on two clocks and in two places, neither of them the point of impact. This was read from code; the audit suggests confirming it by firing at a shielded bot standing in front of a wall.

The reference cases say this class of fault is enough, alone, to make correct combat feel wrong. Riot found during Valorant's 2020 beta that hit-registration complaints spiked when hit effects became less distinct although few real bugs existed: "the visual feedback of the shot was presenting a misleading outcome to the player" ([Riot](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/dev/the-state-of-hit-registration/)). Its fix in Patch 1.07 was two clearly different tiers: a small predicted spark at the bullet's world impact that explicitly "DOES NOT mean your shot has registered", and a server-confirmed effect attached to the character at the body part hit ([Riot](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/game-updates/valorant-patch-notes-1-07/)). Respawn gives each target state its own sound in Apex, for hitting a shield, breaking it, hitting flesh and the finishing blow, and says "it breaks the realism but it makes the game feel so much better to play" ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/apex-legends-sound/)). Halo Infinite shows shield state on the enemy model itself ([GamerGuides](https://gamerguides.com/halo-infinite/guide/multiplayer/getting-started/managing-and-capitalizing-on-ttk)), and 343 still conceded after its first season that "the shield FX could better communicate the opponent's shield state" ([Halo Waypoint](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/season-1-outcomes-report)). Team Fortress 2 pitches its hit sound by damage and plays a separate last-hit sound ([TF2 Wiki](https://wiki.teamfortress.com/wiki/Hit_Sound)).

Being shot is similarly quiet. A shield hit draws a gold edge rim at 0.09 alpha and one 56-by-6-pixel direction arc that lasts 0.6 s and is replaced by the next hit (`crates/client/src/hud.rs:58-68`, `1187-1196`, `1494-1511`); the attacker's tracer, flash and gunshot are held for the render tick, so the damage is felt before its cause is seen or heard (`crates/client-net/src/core.rs:1640-1648`). Removing the camera flinch on 9 November matches the trend, since Halo 5, Black Ops Cold War and Apex all removed or neutered aim-moving flinch ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/flinch-being-removed-from-halo-5/), [Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/apex-legends/apex-legends-players-ecstatic-over-season-22s-uncanny-aim-flinch-change-2847702)). It did, though, remove the loudest "I am being hit" cue without replacing it. 343's replacement in Infinite was audio: a mix that ranks every gun by threat each frame so the one firing at the listener is heard ([Halo Waypoint](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)).

The confirming measurement is a frame-stepped capture of one shielded kill at 120 fps or more: count frames from click to flash, to hitmarker, to flare, and note where each spark lands. The fixes need no new wire fields by my reading, since the hit event already carries the body part and the discarded shot event already carries the true end. They are: distinct marker and sound states for shield hit, shield break, health hit and head; the confirmed impact drawn on the target; the predicted wall mark made visibly a tracer's end rather than a hit; a per-attacker direction arc; and the kill cam that already exists.

One candidate could not be ranked at all. Sound-design guides attribute a weak-feeling gun to a missing layer, a sharp transient without a body or a body without a transient ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-weapon-sounds/)), and 343 builds every Halo Infinite weapon from four layers with the player's own gun mixed punchier than everyone else's ([Halo Waypoint](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/inside-infinite-march-2021)). A code audit can see that oneshot's own fire sound plays centred at gain 0.75 in the frame of the shot (`crates/client/src/sound/mod.rs:59`, `1196-1201`); it cannot hear whether the samples have those layers. Someone has to listen.

## A sidearm at half the rifle's kill time, a three-punch melee and frags that leave shields up

Every player spawns with a BR and a Magnum (`world.rs:69`), and the sidearm is the better gun. The Magnum deals 28 a shot every 180 ms: **five shots and 0.72 s with a finishing headshot**, 1.26 s to the body alone, which is still quicker than the BR's perfect 1.29 s (`arms.rs:351-368`; `crates/sim/tests/range.rs:290-294`). It out-damages the rifle 156 to 74 points a second, has the tighter cone (0.088° against 0.132°) and no falloff to 110 m (derived from the same lines). This is not Halo 2's weapon: Halo 2's multiplayer Magnum does 5.5 a shot, so by arithmetic it needs 13 shots to cross a 70 shield from a 12-round magazine, and its auto-aim reaches 12 world units against the BR's 17 ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/H2:Magnum)). It is a finisher. Bungie's method, in attendee notes on Jaime Griesemer's 2010 talk, allows at most one weapon per role ([attendee notes](https://gamrconnect.vgchartz.com/post.php?id=3259176)), and 343 removed the Magnum as a spawn weapon from Reach's Zero Bloom playlists in favour of the Assault Rifle ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Halo:_Reach_Title_Updates)). Notably, the Magnum's 0.72 s is inside the band mouse-led shooters use. The inference is that a player who finds the pistol experiences the rifle as weak, and one who does not experiences fights as slow. Replays would show it as kill share and swap rate by gun.

Melee and grenades show the opposite error: their Halo points were copied onto a body 74% larger. `MELEE_DMG` is 70, the same figure as Halo 3's tag, but against 200 points it is 35% of a body and three hits to kill (`consts.rs:173`; `crates/sim/tests/range.rs:357-360`). Halo 3's 70 is exactly one 70-point shield, and Halo 2's is 75 at a run and 55 standing, against 115 in total; two blows kill ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/H3:Melee), [Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/H2:Melee), [Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Melee)). By arithmetic on those tags, three BR bursts and a melee kill in Halo 2 and Halo 3; in oneshot it takes five bursts and a melee (150 + 70), by which point four bursts and a headshot have already done the job, so the punch is never the faster finish (derived). The lunge, the carry-over into health and the back-smack are all present (`consts.rs:176-185`; `world.rs:2470-2483`); only the number is out of proportion. By my arithmetic a value of 100 restores the two-hit kill, and 110 restores "three bursts, then punch".

The frag has the same fault. Its 160 points, halved on shields, are Halo 2's exactly (`consts.rs:223-237`; [Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/H2:Frag_Grenade)), but 80 breaks Halo's 70-point shield and leaves oneshot's 100-point shield standing with 20, as the repo's own test states (`crates/sim/tests/combat.rs:1269-1285`). Halo's "grenade first, then the headshot" opener does not exist here; it needs `GREN_DMG` of 200 or more at the current shield factor. Bungie raised grenade damage in Halo 2's 1.1 update because grenades were considered too weak ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Halo_2_Auto-Updates)). Two smaller divergences sit beside it. A sticky's blast on an unstuck full-shield fighter appears to strip the shield exactly and do no health damage, against the constant's own comment that it kills (`consts.rs:253-254`; inferred from `world.rs:2470-2483`, no test). And the round that breaks a shield never kills even on the head (`world.rs:2464`), where Halopedia's Halo CE pistol page says the shield-breaking shot, if a headshot, still kills ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/CE:Pistol)).

The loop around the fight leans the same way. Respawn is 3 s and the shield delay is 5 s, with no spawn protection (`consts.rs:127-132`), and each kill hands the killer a frag (`world.rs:2560`, `2572`). In a duel to 15, the winner of a close exchange, often left unshielded, meets a full-shield opponent two seconds before their own recharge starts, on arenas whose spawn pairs sit a median 35 to 54 m apart, with a rifle that has no falloff to 160 m (derived from the map files and `arms.rs:330-350`). Halo 2 through Halo 4 respawn in 5 s, which in Halo 2 equals the shield delay, Halo 5 Slayer in 8 s ([Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org/Custom_game)), and Destiny 2 raised its 3v3 timer from 5 s to 7 s in 2024 ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/139002/destiny-2-update-7-3-5-patch-notes)). Whether this punishes winning in practice is an inference that replays can test: record the killer's shield at the next exchange.

## Bots, frame queues and peeks: what to measure before tuning anything else

What a solo tester mostly fights is a fill-bot, and the bot's fight is not a person's. Every bot seat runs at skill 0.35, or 0.25 in Siege (`consts.rs:586-592`). It fires only after about 484 ms on a target, a timer that resets whenever sight is lost for one 31 ms look; it aims at the chest, never the head; its aim wobbles with a standard deviation of about 1.4° and trails a change of direction; and it strafes at full speed in straight legs of 0.5 to 1.5 s without crouching (`bot.rs:602-606`, `700-707`, `751-764`; derived figures from those lines). Its best case with a BR is the seven-burst body kill of 2.5 s or more. Fights against bots are therefore long body-shot exchanges against a target crossing the view at 35° a second at 10 m, which the player must track unaided: rank 1's problem in its purest form, plus an opponent that never threatens a fast kill. This is certain as behaviour and unknown as a share of the complaint. The same build played human against human, and logged bot accuracy, would separate the two.

Hand-to-eye latency is the largest unmeasured quantity. What oneshot controls it does well: the camera turns every rendered frame from raw accumulated mouse motion with no smoothing (`crates/client/src/lib.rs:1017-1028`), which is what Bevy's own example prescribes ([Bevy](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/examples/movement/physics_in_fixed_timestep.rs)), and flash, tracer, sound and viewmodel kick all play in the frame the shot is predicted (`crates/client/src/lib.rs:666-724`). What it leaves at engine defaults is the display path. The desktop build defaults to vsync with no cap (`crates/client/src/settings.rs:54`, `121-124`), enables Bevy's multi-threaded feature without disabling pipelined rendering (`crates/client/Cargo.toml:73`; `crates/client/src/lib.rs:462-478`) and sets no frame-queue limit (`crates/client/src/lib.rs:469-476`). Bevy documents a default of 2 queued frames and a pipeline that renders frame N while N+1 simulates ([Bevy](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.18.0/crates/bevy_window/src/window.rs), [Bevy](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/main/crates/bevy_render/src/pipelined_rendering.rs)). The research note's arithmetic makes that 3 to 5 frames, 50 to 83 ms at 60 Hz, before the display; it is inference, and whether pipelining is active was not verified.

In a browser every frame is paced by `requestAnimationFrame` (`crates/client/src/display.rs:13-15`), Chrome delivers mouse moves once per frame ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/aligning-input-events)), and a page cannot control the presentation queue ([James Darpinian](https://james-darpinian-com.pages.dev/blog/latency-platform-considerations)). The page does request unaccelerated mouse input, except on Linux, where OS acceleration can leak in (`crates/client/web/boot.js:12-32`). The stakes are published: in a 68-player CS:GO study, removing 100 ms of local latency improved accuracy by about 6 percentage points against about 2 for the same cut in network latency, which the authors summarise as local latency having about twice the impact ([WPI and Intel](https://web.cs.wpi.edu/~claypool/papers/csgo-net-local-21/paper.pdf)), and Nvidia measured flick scores roughly halving between 25 and 85 ms of system latency ([Nvidia](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/improving-player-performance-with-low-latency-as-evident-from-fps-aim-trainer-experiments/)). A high-speed camera on each build answers it in an afternoon; the levers are the present mode, a frame queue of 1 and a frame pacer.

The netcode is the least likely cause, because its paper numbers match or beat every game compared. Oneshot simulates at 128 Hz and sends snapshots at 64 Hz (`consts.rs:5-8`; `crates/room/src/lib.rs:59-60`). Valorant runs 128 Hz ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/peeking-valorants-netcode)), Halo Infinite 60 Hz for 4v4 and 30 Hz for Big Team ([Halo Waypoint](https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/closer-look-halo-infinite-online-experience)), CS2 64 Hz with sub-tick timestamps ([Team Dignitas](https://dignitas.gg/articles/how-cs2-processes-your-shots-a-guide-to-sub-tick-and-server-refresh-rates)) and Apex 20 Hz ([EA](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/news/servers-netcode-developer-deep-dive)). Hits are server-decided with a rewind capped at 156 ms (`crates/room/src/lib.rs:48-49`, `95-103`), tighter than Counter-Strike's 200 ms ([TotalCSGO](https://totalcsgo.com/commands/svmaxunlag)).

The residue is two floors and one edge case. Other players are drawn at least 4 ticks (31 ms) in the past, up to 94 ms under jitter, coming back down at only one tick a second (`crates/client-net/src/core.rs:325-329`, `1421-1428`), where Valorant's default is 7.8 ms ([Riot](https://playvalorant.com/en-gb/news/game-updates/04-on-peeker-s-advantage-ranked/)). And one to 2.5 input frames wait at the server (`crates/client-net/src/core.rs:2748-2763`), where Riot targets half a frame ([Riot](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/peeking-valorants-netcode)). By the audit's arithmetic a hitmarker lands a round trip plus 8 to 67 ms after the click, and a peeker is seen 47 to 75 ms late even at zero ping. That is comparable with the 40 to 70 ms Riot reports as Valorant's average peeker's advantage ([Riot](https://playvalorant.com/en-gb/news/game-updates/04-on-peeker-s-advantage-ranked/)), in a game where Riot's testers felt 10 ms because kills are near-instant; my inference is that oneshot's long kill hides the same delay better. The fog of war reveals an enemy on a look-ahead of half the round trip plus a snapshot plus the playout delay (`crates/room/src/fog.rs:33-39`), which the audit calculates could show a fast peeker a late pop above roughly 110 to 145 ms of round trip. None of these was observed. The net graph's "interp" line (`crates/client/src/hud.rs:1438-1444`) on browser against desktop, a per-shot log of rewind depth, and Bungie's practice of giving testers a "that felt laggy" button ([Wolfire](https://www.wolfire.com/blog/2011/03/GDC-Session-Summary-Halo-networking)) would close the question.

## Conclusion

The audit changes the question from "which number is wrong" to "which unlisted system did the port leave behind". Oneshot pinned in tests what Halo publishes, the counts, cadences and shield timers, and those match. Halo's feel lives in what the stat card omits: bullets bent toward the target, a strafe acceleration its developers keep retuning, a shield break staged as the loudest event of the fight, and melee and grenade values set against the shield rather than in absolute points. Each of those is off, replaced by Quake's, under-signalled or out of proportion here. That leaves a design decision the evidence cannot make for the operator: give the mouse the help Halo gives it, which starts with one constant, or follow the only commercial mouse-led Halo-like and shorten the kill instead.

Either way the order of work is instrument, then tune. Per-gun accuracy and realised kill time from replays, a frame-stepped capture of one kill, and click-to-photon on each build would turn most rows of the ranking from inference into measurement within days, and they cost less than one tuning pass made blind. Given that the combat model is a week old and three of its feel-defining pieces changed in the last three days, the most valuable single fact may simply be the date the operator first felt it.
